Project Atlas is a fictional Mars mission used as a test document. The Atlas rover was built in Bremen and launched on 14 March 2031. It travels for 207 days before reaching Mars and lands in the Hesperia crater. The rover has six wheels and weighs 640 kilograms. Its power comes from a nuclear battery that delivers 110 watts. The main camera is called Argus and takes images with a resolution of 20 megapixels. A drill on the robotic arm can reach 2 metres below the surface. The mission is led by Dr. Helena Voss, and the communications team is based in Darmstadt. Data is sent to Earth twice per day through the Hermes relay satellite. The planned mission length is 687 days, which equals one Martian year. The rover's top speed is 4 centimetres per second. Its onboard software is written mainly in Rust and runs on a radiation-hardened processor. The mission budget is 1.2 billion euros. The first scientific goal is to search for traces of ancient water in rock samples.
